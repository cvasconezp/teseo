Four ways to explore the universe

Teseo is a planetarium, an interplanetary navigator, a sky calendar and a lab, all at once. It doesn’t just tell you what’s up there: it works out distances, routes and departure dates with real physics and official data.

Explore the questions

Your sky, not just any sky

Most planetariums draw a flat horizon, the same for Quito as for Madrid. Teseo computes yours.

  • Real horizon. The terrain up to 150 km around you, with the curvature of the Earth: you’ll know when the Sun or the Moon actually appears over your mountain, not just the textbook rising time.
  • Layers that add up. Constellations, the Sun and planets, meteor showers, comets, probes such as Voyager, forecast clouds and the Space Station when it passes over.
  • Andean astronomy. Where the Sun rises over your mountains at the solstices and equinoxes and, near Quito, the Quitu-Caranqui sites according to Cristóbal Cobo (2012).
  • 360° photos and postcards with data credits, ready to share.

How Teseo knows what it says is true

Official data and real physics

Ephemerides checked against the US Naval Observatory, NASA and open catalogues. Every number says where it comes from.

AI narrates, it never calculates

When the app uses artificial intelligence, it only describes results the physics has already worked out.

Your location is yours

No account, no sign-up. Your location is used to compute your sky, not to profile you.

Open source

AGPL-3.0 licence and automated tests: anyone can review, use and improve the code, and improvements stay open.

Made in Cayambe, at latitude zero

Teseo was born for the workshops of Killa Ñan, the community astronomy initiative working in Cayambe, Ecuador, since 2018. Today amateur astronomers, teachers and curious people use it.

  • For your group: the sky calendar for your city, subscribed on every member’s phone.
  • For the classroom: the Questions tours work on a projector and on a phone, with nothing to install.
  • For field trips: the time the Sun or the Moon appears over your mountains, and the horizon with its names.
