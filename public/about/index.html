What it is

A thread to find your way in the universe

Teseo is a planetarium, an interplanetary navigator, a sky calendar and a lab, all at once. It doesn’t just tell you what’s up there: it works out distances, routes and departure dates with real physics and official data.

It was born in Cayambe, at latitude zero, out of a need at Killa Ñan: to tell and share astronomy better from a single tool. This page follows its thread: from where you stand to the end of the universe.

Teseo in one minute: the sky map, your horizon, trips across the solar system and the big questions, filmed in the app itself. 44 seconds.

The videos are narrated in Spanish with synthetic voices; the app itself is also available in English.

1 · The thread starts at your feet

Your sky, not just any sky

Most planetariums draw a flat horizon, the same for Quito as for Madrid. Teseo computes yours.

  • Real horizon. Your mountains in 3D, with their names and the time the Sun or Moon rises behind them.
  • Monuments. Cochasquí, Machu Picchu, Teotihuacan and dozens more on their landscape.
  • Your weather. Temperature, wind and clouds by altitude, to know when to go out and look.
  • Layers. Planets, comets, probes, galaxies, pulsars, exoplanets and light pollution.

5 · We weave the thread together

Hecho en Cayambe, en la latitud cero

Teseo was born out of a need at Killa Ñan, the community astronomy initiative working in Cayambe since 2018: to tell and share astronomy better from a single tool. Today amateurs, teachers and curious people use it.

  • For your group: the sky calendar for your city, subscribed on every member’s phone.
  • For the classroom: the Questions tours work on a projector and on a phone, with nothing to install.
  • For field trips: the time the Sun or the Moon appears over your mountains, and the horizon with its names.
